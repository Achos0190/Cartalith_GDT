What makes it loud

  1. Everything is a text chip, at one weight. Each item is a button labelled with its full date, its title and often the date again. Points and ranges look the same, and filters, badges and data share one chip style.
  2. No geometry. The rows are a sorted wrap of chips, not positions on time. A cluster of events in one decade and a gap of five centuries look the same.
  3. The track does not reach the events. The scrubber runs -400 to 1200 by default, so every imported event lies past its right end and the cursor rests at "end of track".
  4. The same event appears up to three times: as a live note row and an imported copy (after Import), and again in the EVENTS row with a source suffix once the cursor moves.
  5. About 15 filter chips are always visible (scope, seven kinds, six factions, export) above the data they filter.
  6. The prompt shows everything at once. Each found timeline carries its full vault path, a three-line preview, a state word, four choice buttons plus Apply, then Re-sync and Compare founding. Import adds an entity menu and an epoch field inline. Choosing and applying are two steps, and the dialog has a large empty area above its footer.

What Chronos does, and what to borrow

The Obsidian Chronos Timeline plugin turns a ```chronos fenced block into an interactive timeline. It is built on chronos-timeline-md, a Markdown wrapper for vis-timeline. Tags: [read] means read in the cited page on 2026-11-22. [recalled] means from memory, unverified.

  • Four item kinds, one character each [read, README]: - an event (a date, or a range with ~), * a point, @ a period (a range drawn as a semi-transparent background band), and = a marker (a single-date milestone). Text after | is the hover description.
  • Groups are swimlanes [read]: {Group Name}. In the Bases view, the "no group" row always sits at the bottom.
  • Colour is opt-in and small [read]: eight named colours (#red and so on). Without one, items take the theme colour.
  • Fit and view [read]: a Refit button (crosshairs, lower right) fits every item. > DEFAULTVIEW start|end sets the opening window, and > HEIGHT n fixes the height (applied automatically from 26 items when a timeline is generated from a folder). > ORDERBY and > GAP control stacking. > NOTODAY hides the "today" bar.
  • Labels and links [read]: an unnamed item is labelled with its date. Hover shows the description, and Ctrl/Cmd-hover previews a linked note. Linked items are underlined, and a click opens the note.
  • Under the hood [read, vis-timeline docs]: item types are box, point, range and background. stack keeps items from overlapping. cluster (with maxItems and a {count} title) merges overlapping items into a count that splits as you zoom. Other options: nestedGroups and showNested for collapsible lanes, fit(), showCurrentTime, and addCustomTime for a draggable time bar.
  • [recalled] A vis-timeline point draws as a dot with its label beside it, and a box as a labelled box on a stem to the axis. Chronos maps = markers to vertical lines across the timeline. I did not find this stated on the pages I read.

Borrowed by all three designs: points look different from ranges; periods become background bands, not items; a fit-to-items view replaces the -400…1200 default; labels are dropped when they collide, never wrapped into a second line of chips; colour is reserved (accent only for the cursor and the selection; source is shown by the shape of a mark, not a hue).

Option C · The ladder (recommended)

This follows the owner's direction: "a minimalist view that's expandable to something more granular up until opening a markdown document with the consolidated cartalith timeline." There are four levels, and each one adds detail. Expanding is a click or tap; collapsing is Back, Esc, or the same chevron.

L0 strip: periods + 5 landmarks→ tap strip / ⌃L1 lanes: points + ranges→ "All 18 events" / tap a clusterL2 table, filterable→ "Open as document"L3 Markdown in Chronos syntax

Back, Esc or ⌄ steps down one level, and the level is remembered per device. Tapping an item at any level moves the year cursor (one write, tl_set_year, as today). Opening a cluster at L1 jumps to L2 filtered to that cluster.

Level 0: the minimal strip

Shows the periods as bands, at most five landmark ticks (conflicts, foundings of capitals, and the vault's = markers), the cursor, and one line of counts. No chips and no filters. The first time a vault timeline is found, it shows as "1 vault timeline new" in the count line, not as a badge.

Level 1: lanes by kind

Cartalith (generated) note, live imported copy same event in both, shown once range period (background band)

Four lanes: Founding, Conflict, Journey, and From the vault. Notes and imported copies share one lane, and the shape of the mark tells them apart. Snapshot and Ownership join only when an item is selected, as the spans lane does today. A label shows only where it fits, and hovering or tapping an item shows its title. An event that is both a live note row and an imported copy is one mark (ring with a dot), not two chips. Filters live behind a single "Filter" button in the strip header, showing the active-filter count.

The proposed default: a generated, read-only export, regenerated from the read model each time it is opened. It is written to one file of its own (for example _cartalith/Consolidated timeline.md), marked by cartalith: generated-timeline in its frontmatter. The writer refuses to overwrite any file without that marker, so it can never replace a user's note. It opens in the vault editor with a read-only banner ("generated; copy a block into your own note to keep it"), and in Obsidian it renders as a live Chronos timeline. Lanes map to {groups}, periods to @, landmarks to =, ranges to - and points to *. Each note event's origin is written as [[link]] after the |, the slot Chronos reads for note linking. Private events are left out (see the questions below).

  • One line per timeline: a status dot, the note name (no path), the event count, and the span or a plain verdict. A timeline already linked says "already live on the timeline", and needs nothing.
  • Expand one at a time. The detail shows a preview on the shared axis: hollow dashed marks show where its events would land, over the filled marks already there. This replaces the three-line text preview and shows duplicates and gaps at a glance.
  • One segmented choice, applied on click, with an Undo toast. That removes the separate Apply. Import and Link open their entity picker only when chosen (marked "…").
  • Advanced actions behind "More": the epoch offset, Re-sync, and Compare founding. Conflicts and duplicates become one sentence ("1 agrees with a founding year · 0 duplicates").
  • The footer keeps one bulk action, matching the design's rule (bulk only for Keep or Ignore), worded as "Leave the rest as they are".

Recommendation

Build C, the ladder. It is the owner's direction. Its L1 uses A's Chronos drawing rules inside lanes, and its L3 is the Chronos export that B also relies on, so A and B stay alternatives without being thrown away.

It is calmer because the resting state (L0) carries no text at all beyond one count line. Detail is one tap away at each step, and nothing is lost: every event is reachable at L2, and the whole timeline is portable at L3.

Suggested order, smallest first: (1) fit the track to the items (Chronos's Refit/DEFAULTVIEW; it fixes the "every event past the end" fault on its own); (2) one mark per event across note and import; (3) L1 lanes; (4) the prompt's single-choice rows; (5) L0; (6) the L2 table; (7) the L3 export.

Fit to the read model's span is the existing design's intent: §2.3 says tl_range() widens to include Founding and visible Imported years. The screenshots show the track not yet doing so for imported years. This is a finding to re-check at the symbol, not a diagnosis.
