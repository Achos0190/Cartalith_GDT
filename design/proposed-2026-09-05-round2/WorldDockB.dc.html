THE CASE FOR B. Sculpting is a different task, and B gives it a
dock. Six sections, all above the fold, instead of six sections under eight headers.
The affordance admits the hiding. B does hide eight categories — and the one
control that says so is pinned where it cannot scroll away, which is the property
a scroll position does not have. It restores the design as written: §2.1 band 2,
§2.3 and §3 rows 1–2 all describe this control, and the two WORLD rail nodes finally
mean what their labels say rather than being two scroll targets.
WHAT B COSTS, HONESTLY. A hidden half is a hidden half. In SCULPT,
eight of nine categories are simply not there, and a user who does not read the
pill concludes the parameters are gone. It is the only gate in the shell.
CIVIL’s eleven categories and CARTO’s four hide nothing, so WORLD’s rule is true
in exactly one of three docks. One more state, reachable three ways — rail node,
pill, arming Sculpt — each of which must agree, which is why the shell needed a
rule that a gate is only allowed where a route exists and a probe to assert it.
And the captions are derived, not read. Whatever is drawn on the pill is this
project’s wording, not the canvas’s — §2.3’s own note says so.
