Paired over the layouts judged in both arms (paired.py here, over the per-town rows; 512 at 384, 643 at 2048):
nearest house median 39 -> 7 m and 38 -> 8 m; the house over the widest street 509 -> 450 of 512 and 642 -> 588 of
643; 460 and 509 towns nearer their bank after. The judged sets differ because more layouts have a house and water
to measure with the switch on (128 -> 42 not judged at 384).

The headline the owner's bar asks for is the house: it is still over the widest street on most layouts, because a
house fronting the embankment stands 1 m clearance + 4 m street + the block inset (half the street plus 1.4 m) off
the channel, about 7 m, against streets of 3.4-5.4 m. Counting the embankment's water side (1 m off the channel by
construction) as the town's frontage, the town reaches its bank on 604 of 618 and 581 of 666 layouts.

Before-gap by river width at 384 (median m, layouts): w17 29.5 (44), w24 42 (245), w31 38 (180), w38 34 (59). It does
not grow with the width, so the doubled half-width is not the dominant term: the 22 m quantisation and the faces that
never close are.

Attribution (site fix only, no embankment): nearest house median 15 m at 384, 17 m at 2048; house or quay
within the street on 185 of 617 at 384. The channel geometry halves the gap; the embankment closes the rest.

Residual at 2048: 85 layouts are still over a street (house or quay); 66 layouts (3 at 384) have no street or lane within 46 m
(EMBANKMENT_REACH) of the river at all, so the embankment has nothing to tie to: the layout's river runs away
from the town (placement), not a setback.
The building gap stays above the widest street by construction: a house fronts the 4 m embankment, so it
stands 1 m + 4 m + the block inset (half the street plus 1.4 m) back from the channel; the quay is at the bank.
