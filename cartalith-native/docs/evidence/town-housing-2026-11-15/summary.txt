Town housing after Ruling EX: the FH-R2 building-count drop, broken down (2026-11-15)

Question (OUTSTANDING_WORK.md, "Town placement" row): fh_r2_baseline's three largest towns' buildings, summed over the 20 harness
worlds, fell 236 747 (3ae0e658) -> 205 261 (head, civ.river_front off) -> 173 439 (head). Which cause, and is it correct?

Tools (both #[ignore]d measurements, assert nothing; run alone in release):
  fh_r2_baseline.rs::fh_r2_urban_breakdown   per town of the harness rows (FH_R2_URBAN_FRONT, _BANK, _ONLY, _DEBUG)
  town_river_census.rs::town_housing_audit   every town of seeds 483920/912345/246371, urban_riverbank off and on
Files: fh_r2_breakdown.txt (configurations A-D and the variants V1, V2, per town), fh_r2_debug_*.txt (DBG lines),
housing_<grid>_head.txt and _variant_V1/_V2.txt (one row per town, switch off | on), viewer_*.png.

1. Split by cause (fh_r2_breakdown.txt; each total reproduces the AT_CURRENT figure):
   A 3ae0e658 behaviour        236 747 buildings, housed 91.2 % of target, per-town median 92 %
   B EX switchless fixes only  205 261 (-31 486)   79.4 %   median 87 %   target unchanged (same 59/60 towns, same pops)
   C + civ.river_front         173 439 (-31 822)   67.3 %   median 73 %   target +1.1 % (more towns at the 20 000 cap)
   pop_target did not fall. No block was dropped as wet in any number that matters: the wet share of a town box went from
   0.00 (A: the river point half a cell south-east, the stem outside the box) to 0.04-0.05 (C: a 30-46 m channel across a
   1.7 km box). What changed is where the river runs: B moves the stem onto the town (traced_to_cell), C moves every big town
   onto a river (river_front + the river constraint): all 59 C towns are 'river' (48) or 'riverthrough' (11) sites with a
   2-6 point real stem through the box (wet share 0.02-0.07). In A, 34 of the 59 had a real stem that never reached the
   box (wet 0.00), so they were laid out as if dry; they housed a median 92 % (37-125 %).

2. Mechanism, read at the symbols and measured (fh_r2_debug_D_app.txt, urban_riverbank on as in the app):
   - generate pins a real-water town's market at the box centre (v0.98/v1.00), which since Ruling EX is on the river's bank.
   - grow's far-bank rule (rule 3, reference): on a 'river' site the far bank grows only within bridgehead_distance (190 m) of
     the bridge, with probability 0.35 - written for the synthetic river laid across the box's lower third, the market
     60-240 m inland (placeAnchors), where the far bank is a strip. With the market on the bank it is half the growth disk.
   - the reference lays no bridge on real water (addRiverBridges returns when usesRealWater): 40 of 60 towns had no
     crossing at all (D), so not even the bridgehead forms. 54 of 60 D towns have 10 % or less of their buildings on the
     second bank (DBG "sides", the centroid's side of the nearest centreline segment).
   - riverbank off (the harness): the bridge point is one of the stem's 2 km-apart vertices, often outside the box; the
     market fallback and primaries then fail. Zero-building towns: 3 in C (404/800/24 #0, 404/40075/6 #6, 404/40075/24 #15),
     1 in A (24601/40075/6 #2), 0 in D.
   So the old counts were partly towns laid out as if their river were not there (the town at (43,34): A_before_EX.png, a
   round walled town with no river in its box), and the new ones are towns on their river that build on one bank only.

3. Verdict: a regression for towns of 5 000 and up. In the app (D), the 60 harness towns house a median 76 % of target
   (p10 46 %, 42 under 80 %), against 92 % before. Across the three census seeds (housing_*_head.txt, riverbank on) towns
   of 5 000 and up house a median 87 % at 384 and 74 % at 2 048 (under 80 %: 9 of 27 and 12 of 23). Villages are the
   opposite: median 139-142 % of target (the over-build already routed as the riverbank row's item (3)).

4. Two fixes built and measured under urban_riverbank, NEITHER SHIPPED:
   V1 far-bank rule lifted when the market stands within 60 m of its river (placeAnchors' nearest distance) + a bridge at the
      bridge point joined to the town: harness 254 312 buildings, housed 96.5 %, median 95 %, 7 under 80 %; census towns
      >= 5 000: under 80 % 9 -> 0 (384), 12 -> 4 (2 048). Rejected on the look: town (43,34) (variant_V1.png) is walled
      across both banks round an empty far-bank interior, the wall crossing the river twice, and a 400-target village at
      2 048 went to 783 buildings (703 %).
   V2 the joined bridge only: harness 191 944, median 76 %, 39 under 80 % (big towns unchanged: under 80 % 9 -> 8, 12 -> 12);
      villages over 200 % rose 119 -> 145 and 143 -> 186. Rejected: it does not fix the regression and worsens the over-build.
   The choice is a design call (owner): let a river town grow on both banks (V1's rule, with the wall made to follow), or keep
   the reference's one-bank town and give it its disk (place the market and box inland of the river, as the synthetic site does).

   direction, different world and kind, so not the same layout. Measured on it: its river is a two-point traced stem that ENDS
   at the marker ((850, 2708) -> (850, 625) in box metres). Off, the bridge point and river port sit at the stem's far vertex,
   2 km outside the box, with no quay: 122 parcels, 5 190 m of street. On, the resampled centreline puts the bridge point at

   (grow: d_m = min(market, quay + 35)), so growth gathers at the box edge, where segments are refused 40 m short of it.
