Cause. None of the 8 was a cheap crossing: every run cell's routing cell was IMPASSABLE, and the charge map writes
impassable as 0, the same value as free. At 2 048 the road router works on a 1 024 x 656 grid; each routing cell
reads ONE full-resolution sample for its water test (the downsampled height under sea, or the sampled cell in a
water body), while civ.map_crossings' charge reads the whole 2 x 2 block. Where a drawn river reaches the shore or a
lake, a block can hold dry river cells while its sample is sea (1520:293: the sample (1520, 294) is ocean) or lake
(343:363: the sample is in lake 2), so the router never enters it; the drawn, smoothed way cuts that corner and
crosses the river on the dry cell, one cell from where the routed path crossed the same river at a charged cell.
The fifth cell (246371 1100:1189) is different: the drawn road there runs over a lake cell, not a crossing.

Fix (inside civ.map_crossings, cartalith-civ civ_routing_cell_passable): with the switch on, a routing cell whose
sample is water but whose block holds a DRY cell on a drawn river (in no water body, not under the sea, order >= 1)
is passable, and its per-cell lookups (flow, order, biome) read that dry river cell, so it is charged as the ford or
bridge it is. One function serves the router (civ_enhanced_travel_cost, both network passes and way_merge's trunk
cost) and the census's charge map (civ_crossing_charge_map). Inert where routing is 1:1 (every map up to RW_CAP =
1 024 wide): the block is the sample, which failed the test.
