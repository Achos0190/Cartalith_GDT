River towns on both banks, with a mixed riverside (Rulings FB and EZ item 1, owner 2026-11-15), under urban_riverbank.

Owner, verbatim. FB: "Big river towns can grow around both banks when the terrain allows it." EZ item 1: "Riverside houses
are fine, as well as having a street in between the river and house. So both conditions can exist (look at for example
Amsterdam)".

Route (DECISIONS.md section 7p): rides WorldParams::urban_riverbank (true in params::defaults, false in
WorldParams::defaults and for a save without the key; read fresh when a town is laid out). The urban goldens and the
FH-R2 / G1 harnesses lay towns out with it off, so none moved.

What changed (cartalith-urban, each new constant labelled in its doc comment):
 1. Both banks. A town of BOTH_BANKS_MIN_POP and up (= plaza::PLAZA_MARKET_POP, 1 500: the urban crate's chartered-town
    line, village below) on a riverbank river site gets a bridge before it grows (riverbank::lay_town_bridge): a 6 m
    primary across the site's bridge point, joined to the nearest street on the market's bank within 240 m, with a 60 m
    approach on the far bank; an existing primary counts only if it crosses at the bridge itself. grow then lifts the
    reference's 190 m bridgehead rule (GrowOpts::far_bank): a far-bank origin must be buildable
    (riverbank::far_bank_buildable: terrain_suitability >= 0.5, so no gorge or marsh bank) and the far bank's streets may
    not exceed the market bank's (FAR_BANK_MAX_RATIO 1.0).
 2. The wall. The wall hug decides from the lots whether the circuit spans the river (wallhug::one_bank_by_lots: the
    smaller bank must hold 32 % of the larger's lots and 8), else it is refitted round the larger bank alone; when that
    one-bank refit cannot meet the 5 m margin in four widenings, the last widening is taken rather than keeping the
    reference circuit round an empty bank.
 3. Riverside frontage. build_embankment alternates 90-220 m stretches of the 4 m embankment quay with 'bank' edges (1 m,
    not drawn) along which the lots front the water; each run's ends are tied to the nearest street node within 46 m so
    it closes its faces; lots fronting either are judged on slope alone (districts::lot_suitability), since the flood
    factor otherwise leaves exactly that row vacant.
 4. Villages. A riverbank village (under 1 500) housing over RIVER_HOUSING_CAP (125 %) of its target empties its
    outermost inland lots, those outside its wall first (riverbank::trim_overbuilt).
 5. Bay towns. Not in this code: the 457 "buildings in water" in six bay towns of seed 483920 at 2 048 were the census
    test's artefact. On a coast or bay layout the 'river' polyline is shore_from_mask's shoreline (land cells beside the
    sea sorted along one axis, zigzagging across the town) and the channel test counted houses within 6 m of it. 0 of
    them are in a sea or lake cell, 0 in water_poly (both_banks_audit's split columns, before and after). The census's
    wet_buildings now applies the channel test to river layouts only; the housing audit reads 0 in water at both sizes.

Reading the table:
- Big towns: the median rises to about target and the under-80 % tail halves; on both banks 4 -> 24 of 33 at 384 and
  1 -> 20 of 34 at 2 048. More walls cross the river because more towns are built on both banks; the ones round an empty
  bank fall (6 -> 3, 14 -> 5). The residue at 2 048 is mostly one-bank circuits whose water wall follows a bend and
  touches the centreline (seed 912345 #20, unchanged by this work), which the audit's crude test cannot tell apart.
- The cap makes the village median exactly 125 %: over half of riverbank villages are trimmed. Villages under 80 %
  are not helped (pre-existing; mostly towns whose layout places few streets).
- The setback: the share of towns with every house over 2 street widths halves at 384 (176 -> 87) and falls by a third at
  2 048 (238 -> 156). The pooled house shares move little because most of a town's houses are inland.
- 'built frontage (a house or a quay's water side) over the widest street' (riverbank_gap_audit) rose 14 -> 42 of 617
  at 384 and 85 -> 112 of 666 at 2 048: where a bank stretch replaces a quay and the lots behind it are not built (too steep), nothing reaches the
  bank. Left open.
